Dates exercises

These exercises go with the dates and times slides. You’ll have about 12 minutes in the session; finish any you don’t get to afterwards. Questions under “Going further” are optional.

They use the HES admissions and patients data from Sessions 2 and 3.

TipStarting here?

Run this code first (see the Session 3 practical for how to set up the project):

library(tidyverse)
library(here)

admissions <- read_csv(here("data", "raw", "hes_admissions.csv"))
patients <- read_csv(here("data", "raw", "hes_patients.csv"))

lubridate is loaded with the tidyverse, so you don’t need to load it separately.

  1. Calculate each patient’s age in whole years when they were admitted. What are the youngest and oldest ages?
TipHint

First join patients to admissions by patient_id to bring in the date of birth. Then use interval(date_of_birth, admission_date) / years(1) and round down with floor().

NoteSolution 
admissions_age <- admissions |>
  left_join(patients, by = "patient_id") |>
  mutate(age = floor(interval(date_of_birth, admission_date) / years(1)))

admissions_age |>
  select(spell_id, date_of_birth, admission_date, age)

  1. Compare discharge_date - admission_date with los_days. Are they the same? Why not?
NoteSolution 
admissions |>
  mutate(stay = as.numeric(discharge_date - admission_date)) |>
  select(spell_id, los_days, stay)
# A tibble: 6,711 × 3
   spell_id los_days  stay
   <chr>       <dbl> <dbl>
 1 S0000001     11.8    12
 2 S0000002     12.7    13
 3 S0000003     11.9    12
 4 S0000004     14.7    15
 5 S0000005     11.6    12
 6 S0000006     11.3    12
 7 S0000007      9.9    10
 8 S0000008     10.7    11
 9 S0000009      6.9     7
10 S0000010      7.6     8
# ℹ 6,701 more rows

los_days has decimals (e.g. 11.8 days), but dates have no times, so the difference between two dates is always a whole number of days. In this data, discharge_date is the admission date plus los_days rounded up:

admissions |>
  mutate(stay = as.numeric(discharge_date - admission_date)) |>
  summarise(
    same = sum(stay == los_days),
    rounded_up = sum(stay == ceiling(los_days))
  )
# A tibble: 1 × 2
   same rounded_up
  <int>      <int>
1   700       6711

Only 700 of the 6,711 spells match exactly, but all of them match once los_days is rounded up.
